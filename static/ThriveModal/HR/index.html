The HR and Recruiting Work You Can Move Offshore, and the Judgment That Stays With You

Recruiters, talent acquisition coordinators, HR generalists, payroll administrators. The operational side of HR (sourcing, screening, scheduling, records, payroll prep) runs just as well from the Philippines as from your local office, hand picked for your team and supported for the long term.

HR is the category where "offshore" makes people uncomfortable. That's fair.

HR holds the most sensitive information in your business: pay, performance, discipline, medical disclosures, Social Security numbers. It runs inside federal and state employment law that changes from one state line to the next. And it involves judgment calls, a hard performance conversation, a termination, an investigation, that don't transfer to a remote arrangement without real thought.

So let's be direct about what offshore HR support is and what it isn't. Your Thrivemodal hire is not your HR director. They're not your employment attorney. They're not running terminations or handling grievances without your oversight.

What they are is the operational engine of your HR and recruiting function: sourcing and screening candidates, scheduling interviews, keeping the ATS clean, maintaining HRIS records, processing onboarding paperwork, preparing payroll, running training logistics. That work eats most of a small HR team's week and very little of its legal judgment. And a skilled, experienced HR professional in the Philippines does it just as well as one sitting in your office.

This page breaks down which HR and recruiting roles make sense to offshore, exactly where the line sits for a US employer, how to protect employee data, and what the Thrivemodal hiring process looks like.

Where the Savings Are

Why the Operational Layer Is Where Offshore HR Delivers the Most Value

Nobody is offshoring the VP of People. The value of that seat is judgment, relationships, culture and knowledge of your state's employment law. That stays local.

The cost argument is about the layer underneath: the recruiter working the pipeline, the coordinator chasing interview times, the administrator reconciling timesheets before payroll closes. Those roles consume most of a small HR team's time and require a fraction of its judgment.

In the US, an HR specialist or recruiter earns a median of $75,940 a year and costs about $108,500 fully loaded once employer taxes and benefits are added. A payroll clerk runs about $83,200 fully loaded, an HR assistant about $72,300 (BLS, May 2025). A dedicated, full-time Thrivemodal hire in the Philippines runs $24,000 to $28,500 a year all-in. For a company carrying two or three of these roles, that's $100,000 or more a year you can move toward the strategic HR work that actually needs a senior local hire.

The Boundary That Matters

What Offshore HR Staff Can and Cannot Do for a US Employer

Employment law is more state-specific than almost anything else a business deals with. Getting this line right before you hire is the single most important part of setting up offshore HR support.

What they cannot do for a US employer

  • Give employment law advice, federal or state
  • Make or deliver decisions on terminations, layoffs, discipline or accommodations
  • Represent you before the EEOC, NLRB, a state labor agency or in litigation
  • Complete Section 2 of Form I-9 (in-person document review is required unless you use E-Verify's remote option)
  • Decide FLSA exempt vs. non-exempt status or contractor classification
  • Approve and submit payroll, sign tax filings or own multi-state registrations
  • Run an investigation or grievance process on their own
  • Act as an HR business partner making people decisions without a US lead

What they can do, and do well

  • Run recruiting end to end at the coordination level: job ads, sourcing, resume screening, first-round screens, scheduling, ATS hygiene and candidate communication
  • Administer onboarding paperwork, approved offer letters, background check vendors and HRIS data entry
  • Prepare payroll: timesheet reconciliation, PTO balances and employee changes, ready for your US reviewer
  • Maintain employee records, files and policy acknowledgments
  • Support performance review cycles administratively
  • Produce HR reporting, dashboards and people analytics
The division is operational execution versus legal and strategic judgment. Your Thrivemodal hire owns the first. Your US HR lead, PEO or employment attorney owns the second. When that line is written down and respected, offshore HR works. When the offshore hire gets pulled into sensitive employee matters because nobody else is around, problems follow.

Personnel Data

Protecting Employee Data: Set It Up Before Anyone Starts

There's no single federal employee privacy law in the US. Applicant and employee data falls under state privacy laws like CCPA/CPRA, FCRA for background checks, HIPAA for self-funded health plans, state SSN protections and breach notification laws in all 50 states. The offshore location doesn't add risk. Skipping the setup does.

Role-scoped system access

Your hire sees only what their job needs. A recruiter needs the ATS, not the compensation module. A payroll admin needs timesheets, not performance reviews. Every major ATS and HRIS supports role-based permissions.

Data obligations in writing

Confidentiality terms that name employee and applicant data specifically: no exporting outside approved systems, no personal email or devices, clear consequences. Generic confidentiality language isn't enough.

Company-managed equipment

Thrivemodal supplies the laptop, so your hire is never working on a personal machine. Personnel data moves only through your HRIS, ATS and company email or chat.

Audit trail review

Your HRIS and ATS log who opened which record and when. Reviewing those logs periodically is good governance for anyone with access to personnel data, wherever they sit.

Avoid the Mistakes

Five Things That Go Wrong When Companies Offshore HR, and How to Avoid Them

Offshore HR and recruiting support has a strong track record when it's set up well. These are the mistakes that show up when it isn't.

No clear line between HR operations and legal judgment

The most common and most expensive mistake. When the offshore hire ends up handling an employee complaint, a wage question or a termination because nobody defined the scope, or nobody local was available, problems follow fast. Write down what the role does and doesn't do, and make sure a US person is reachable for the rest.

Treating the offshore recruiter as the decision-maker

Screening without written criteria, quoting salary without approval, sending offers nobody signed off on. Offshore recruiting works as pipeline and process management. Give them a scorecard, define what gets escalated and keep hiring decisions with your managers.

No documented HR processes before you hire

An offshore administrator can only follow processes that exist on paper. If HR lives in two people's heads, the new hire will either improvise or ask constantly. Document the onboarding checklist, employee change workflow, PTO process and offboarding steps first. You need them anyway.

Payroll and personnel data without access controls

Full salary register access, export rights or visible SSNs with no business need is a governance gap, wherever the person sits. Role-scope it. Mask it. Log it.

Expecting your context to come with the hire

An experienced Filipino HR professional knows a lot about US HR conventions. They don't know your state's leave rules, your industry's hiring norms or your company's culture until you show them. Build that into onboarding on purpose.

FAQ

Common Questions Before Getting Started

Straight answers to what US owners and HR managers ask before offshoring HR work, including the harder questions about employment law, data and what happens when things get sensitive.

Can an offshore HR professional work within FLSA, state wage law and EEO requirements?
With caveats. Experienced professionals who've supported US companies are fluent in US HR conventions and documentation. What they can't do is give legal advice, make compliance calls or handle legally sensitive matters without your oversight. Think operational fluency, not legal authority.
How do we protect employee data when someone offshore is in our HRIS?
Role-based access in your HRIS, confidentiality terms that name employee data specifically, company-managed equipment, approved channels only and periodic audit log review. The same governance you'd apply to any employee with access to personnel data.
Can an offshore HR generalist handle employee relations?
Routine administration around employee relations, yes: keeping the records of a process your US lead is running. Handling complaints, investigations, discipline or terminations on their own, no. They need a clear, reachable escalation path.
What about recruiting for senior or confidential roles?
The offshore recruiter or coordinator can still run the administrative workflow: scheduling, communication, ATS. Sourcing strategy, assessment and offer negotiation for those roles should stay with your team.
Can they complete Form I-9 for our new hires?
Not Section 2. It requires the employer or an authorized representative to examine documents in person, unless you're enrolled in E-Verify and use the DHS remote verification option. Your hire can prepare everything around it.
Does the hire work for us exclusively?
Yes. Every placement is a dedicated, full-time member of your team. Thrivemodal is the employer of record and handles payroll, benefits, HR and Philippines compliance; you direct the work day to day.
What if the hire isn't working out?
Most early issues come from gaps in the brief or onboarding, and the fix is documentation and feedback. If a skill or quality issue persists past onboarding, the Zero Risk Guarantee applies: we replace the hire at no cost.
