Why Hire an Offshore Recruitment Specialist?

Recruiting the right people takes time, consistency and follow-through. For most growing US companies, building an in-house recruiting team isn't practical, and asking hiring managers to source and screen on top of their day job means roles sit open.

An offshore Recruitment Specialist gives you full-time recruiting capacity: sourcing, screening and shortlisting candidates against your criteria, so your managers spend their time interviewing finalists instead of reading resumes. Filipino recruiters have supported US hiring for well over a decade and know US candidate communication norms, ATS platforms and the rhythm of US hiring cycles.

It is also a strong fit for staffing and recruiting firms that need sourcing and screening horsepower behind their account managers.

Key Responsibilities of an Offshore Recruitment Specialist

  • Write, post and refresh job ads across Indeed, LinkedIn, ZipRecruiter and niche boards
  • Source passive candidates with Boolean, LinkedIn Recruiter and resume databases
  • Screen resumes and run structured first-round phone or video screens
  • Schedule, confirm and reschedule interviews across hiring manager calendars
  • Keep ATS stages, notes and dispositions current and clean
  • Manage candidate communication from application through offer paperwork
  • Coordinate background checks and references through your vendors
  • Report on pipeline, time to fill, source of hire and funnel conversion
The Boundary

What They Can and Cannot Do for a US Employer

Operational execution sits with your Thrivemodal hire. Legal and strategic judgment stays with you, your US HR lead or your counsel. Here is where that line falls for this role.

Cannot

  • Decide who advances to final rounds or who gets the offer
  • Communicate an offer, salary or terms not approved in writing
  • Negotiate compensation without explicit authority and a range
  • Screen on judgment without written criteria (that creates EEO exposure)
  • Lead strategy on confidential or executive searches
  • Set your employer brand voice (they execute the voice you give them)

Can

  • Source and engage passive candidates
  • Screen and phone screen against written criteria you set
  • Post and optimize job ads
  • Run interview scheduling end to end
  • Prepare and send approved offer letters and collect signatures
  • Keep the ATS audit-ready with complete dispositions
The offshore recruiter runs the process and the pipeline. Who advances to the final round and who gets the offer stays with your hiring managers.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

How does an offshore recruiter cut time to fill?
By keeping a warm pipeline before the req opens and sourcing during US evening hours, so screened candidates are waiting when your hiring manager logs on in the morning.
Can they handle technical or niche searches?
Yes at the sourcing and first-screen level, using Boolean searches and a knockout list written by your hiring manager. Deep assessment of a niche skill stays with your team or a technical interviewer.
Do they manage the full cycle, including offers?
They run the whole workflow, including preparing an approved offer letter and collecting signatures. The decision to offer, the number and any negotiation authority come from you, in writing, first.
How is candidate experience protected?
Templated, timely communication at every stage, rejections included, so nobody is left hanging. Most clients see candidate experience improve over an overloaded in-house process.
What about EEO and fair hiring?
Structured screens, consistent criteria and complete ATS dispositions make your process more defensible, not less. The recruiter applies the criteria you design and documents everything.
Can they work in our ATS and our job board accounts?
Yes. They work inside your systems with role-scoped access you control. We screen for hands-on experience in the platforms you already run.
Do they make cold calls to candidates?
Yes, during US hours. Phone and video outreach is standard, and clear, professional spoken English is one of the first things we screen for.
