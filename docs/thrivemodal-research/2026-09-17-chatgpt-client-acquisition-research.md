Thrivemodal: client acquisition research and AI targeting playbook
Research snapshot: September 17, 2026

## The sales recommendation
Build the prospecting engine around a recurring business workload, a visible reason to act, and the person responsible for that workload. An industry label is only the beginning. A property manager with a growing portfolio and an open coordination role is a more useful lead than an arbitrary company classified as real estate.

Start with logistics, accounting and property management. Thrivemodal identifies these as its deepest experience areas. Home care has dedicated company content; marketing has a named placement story. Expand into adjacent markets only as explicitly labeled experiments. [Homepage](https://thrivemodal.com/)

The accompanying JSON contains the canonical fact records, source registry, market rules and exclusions. Company-published facts mean that the claim was found on a company source; they do not mean the result was independently audited. All filters, account sizes, buyer selections, scoring weights, campaign ideas and recommended KPIs below are analyst proposals.

## Positioning to use
Suggested sales framing: help a business create capacity for recurring work, with the person and support structure needed to make the arrangement sustainable. Start the conversation with the workflow that is slowing growth. Discuss cost after the role, quality requirements and workload are clear.

Avoid positioning every opportunity as a generic virtual assistant. A buyer responsible for shipment exceptions, month-end preparation or maintenance coordination needs to understand the actual work the person would own.

## Market priorities
Size ranges are proposed starting filters, not Thrivemodal minimums or verified customer profiles. Search outside them when workload evidence is stronger.

### 1. Freight brokerages and 3PLs
- **Evidence:** core. US; test 20–250 employees.
- **Likely buyers:** Owner/President, COO, VP Operations, Director of Brokerage.
- **Pain to investigate:** evening shipment updates; document chasing; exception queues.
- **Suggested first workload:** track-and-trace desk; POD follow-up; operations support.
- **Signals to research:** current operations vacancy; new shipper contract; new branch; extended service hours.
- **Discovery question:** Which shipment tasks pull your domestic team away from customers?
- **Proposed next step:** Map one recurring shipment queue and its escalation rules.
- **Measure:** loads handled per shift; update timeliness; document turnaround.
- **Qualification boundary:** Confirm TMS proficiency, shift requirements and exception authority.
- **Do not overread:** active job posting alone does not prove understaffing.
- **Source basis:** [Homepage](https://thrivemodal.com/), [Services](https://thrivemodal.com/services/), [Global Talent Advantage landing page](https://info.thrivemodal.com/global-talent-advantage). The targeting interpretation is a hypothesis.

## What makes an account worth researching
Use two layers: enduring fit and current timing.

Enduring fit asks whether a meaningful, repeated workflow can be performed remotely; whether the company has an internal manager; and whether it can provide documented procedures, system access and quality review. Timing asks what has changed recently to make staffing capacity relevant.

A job advertisement proves the company advertised a role. It does not prove that the vacancy remains open, has been hard to fill, or would be considered for offshore staffing. Open the employer’s current career page before mentioning the job in outreach. Similarly, an acquisition suggests a workflow to investigate; it does not prove an integration problem.

### Signal quality
| Signal | Use | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Current employer-hosted role posting | Tie a question to the advertised work | Claiming the company cannot hire |
| Announced new location, contract or portfolio | Explore new workload and handoffs | Inventing revenue or volume increases |
| Public statement by an operations leader | Reference the exact operational priority | Rewriting it as a budget commitment |
| Repeated dated complaints about response | Treat as a weak research clue needing corroboration | Accusing the prospect of poor service |
| Funding or acquisition | Investigate operational priorities | Assuming available budget or intent |
| Documented software/workflow | Assess remote readiness | Claiming Thrivemodal has a certified integration |
| Generic industry membership | Establish category only | Treating membership as a buying signal |
| Prospect explicitly requests staffing help | Prioritize direct qualification | Assuming price, scope or procurement approval |

Recommended freshness windows: verify vacancies within 30 days; prioritize announcements within 90 days; retain older evidence as background with its date. These are research heuristics.

## Lead scoring that remains honest
Use the JSON scoring rubric as a prioritization aid. It is not a conversion probability.

| Dimension | Maximum | Full-score evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Workload fit | 30 | Specific recurring work matches a published function |
| Documented trigger | 25 | Recent direct evidence connected to that work |
| Remote delivery readiness | 20 | Systems, workflow and internal management are verified |
| Recurring volume and economics | 15 | Enough sustained work and a plausible budget, confirmed |
| Buyer access | 10 | A current relevant buyer or clear contact route is verified |

Award half points when evidence is partial, zero provisional points when unknown, and mark unknown separately. Do not give maximum points merely because an industry sounds suitable. Evidence coverage is the sum of maximum weights for criteria with usable evidence divided by 100. A low score with low coverage should trigger more research.

Use A = 75–100, B = 55–74, C = below 55, with hard exclusions overriding the number. Explicit inability to use offshore access or a role requiring physical presence may rule out that workload. A low score should not automatically label the whole company a bad fit.

## Sales conversations and objections
These are suggested discovery responses, not new contractual promises.

| Buyer concern | Useful response direction | Next question |
|---|---|---|
| “We need industry knowledge.” | Specify scenarios candidates must handle and assess them during selection | What would a strong candidate know by the first interview? |
| “We tried outsourcing before.” | Diagnose the earlier failure: scope, management, communication or role fit | Where did the arrangement first break down? |
| “I cannot manage another person.” | Test whether the client has capacity to train and own the workflow | Who can provide day-to-day priorities and review? |
| “Our customers expect someone local.” | Separate communication quality from tasks that require presence | Which conversations truly require someone on-site? |
| “Security is a concern.” | Review access, data and required controls before proposing the role | What requirements would the arrangement need to satisfy? |
| “How much will we save?” | Build a like-for-like estimate using the actual quote and workload | What does the comparable local capacity cost in total? |
| “We need coverage around the clock.” | Build a staffed schedule, handoffs and escalation plan | Which hours, volumes and service levels need coverage? |
| “We only need a few hours.” | Confirm whether Thrivemodal offers a suitable engagement | Is there enough adjacent recurring work to define a useful role? |
| “We already have offshore staff.” | Investigate an uncovered workload or a new skill requirement | What capacity is still missing from your current team? |

One person does not provide continuous 24/7 coverage. Do not confuse coverage availability with a complete staffed schedule. Ongoing staffing support also does not eliminate the client’s need to define work and manage priorities.

## Proof and claims discipline
Knichel Logistics and Travel Byrds should be treated as named proof references, not prospect accounts. Do not infer that either needs additional services. The public Travel Byrds summary concerns a marketing leadership hire; it does not establish travel booking expertise or a numerical revenue result. [Travel Byrds summary](https://info.thrivemodal.com/travelbyrds)

Keep these claims behind explicit qualification:
- The company’s savings percentage is an upper-bound marketing claim. Use actual quote comparisons for an account.
- Company-reported award language should remain attributed until the award issuer’s record is verified.
- Do not invent retention rates, number of clients, number of placements, guarantee exclusions or software credentials.
- Published security practices do not establish a certification, legal conclusion or suitability for every data category.
- A listed CPA role does not establish licensure in a particular US jurisdiction.
- Do not reproduce broad claims about what all competitors do. Compare actual proposals.
- Avoid national stereotypes. Assess each candidate’s communication, skill and experience directly.

## Campaign ideas to test
Each campaign is a proposed message angle, not an existing Thrivemodal product or approved promotion.

1. **Capacity behind the vacancy.** Identify a current role posting; ask which recurring tasks need ownership. Offer a role-mapping conversation.
2. **The work-order handoff.** Target property managers adding portfolios; offer a map of request intake, vendor scheduling and completion follow-up.
3. **Protect partner review time.** Target accounting firms expanding CAS; identify preparation work that reaches senior staff unnecessarily.
4. **The evening shipment queue.** Target logistics operations with verified extended hours; discuss handoffs and exception ownership.
5. **Intake without office overload.** Target home care agencies adding branches; focus on administrative coordination.
6. **One workflow first.** For offshore newcomers, scope a bounded recurring responsibility with a manager, baseline and review plan. Do not advertise a free trial.
7. **Specialist capacity.** For firms already using assistants, investigate a more defined operational or marketing role; confirm recruitment feasibility.
8. **Acquisition workload.** Use a verified acquisition to ask about duplicated administration and reporting, without asserting integration trouble.
9. **Founder time audit.** For smaller companies, identify recurring administrative hours that displace revenue-producing work; check dedicated-role economics.
10. **Referral partners.** Explore operational consultants, fractional finance leaders and advisors serving the target vertical. Referral terms would need separate agreement.

Test one vertical, buyer and workload at a time. Track relevant replies, qualified conversations, opportunities, hires and retention of resulting customers. Open rates alone are not success. Compare reasons for rejection before expanding lists.

## A useful cost comparison
Use an internal scenario calculator, not a blanket savings promise.

Annual local equivalent cost = salary + employer costs + benefits + incremental equipment/software + recruitment cost allocated to the period.

Annual offshore equivalent cost = 12 × quoted monthly fee + incremental client costs not included in that quote + onboarding/management time valued consistently.

Estimated annual savings = local equivalent cost − offshore equivalent cost.
Estimated savings percentage = estimated savings ÷ local equivalent cost × 100.

Compare equal hours, skill, responsibilities and review requirements. Avoid counting included costs twice. Mark unknown inputs as missing; do not insert invented fees. For a vacancy, distinguish budget savings from recovered capacity: an unfilled position may have no current salary expense. Any additional revenue estimate requires an explicit business model and should be a scenario, never a promised result.

## How the website should use this memory
1. Collect a market in plain language. Normalize it into a segment and recurring workflow.
2. Ask only for missing details that materially change results: geography, workload, size, lead count or exclusions.
3. Retrieve matching market records plus the relevant factual claims and source records.
4. If the market is new, map it to published functions and mark the vertical as exploratory.
5. Research current accounts if live sources are available. If not, return a search plan; never fabricate a lead list.
6. Store observations separately from pain hypotheses.
7. Score fit, show missing evidence, select a plausible buyer role and propose a discovery question.
8. Draft outreach only from verified account evidence and permitted company claims.
9. Return source links and evidence dates with every actual account.
10. Save new verified evidence with source and date; do not promote generated suggestions into permanent facts.

Keep permanent company knowledge separate from changing prospect evidence and the user’s preferences. Store the research date so older commercial terms can be refreshed. A generated hypothesis should remain a hypothesis across later sessions.
